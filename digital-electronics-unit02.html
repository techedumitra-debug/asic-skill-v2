RELATED TOPIC · DECIMAL

2.1 · The Decimal Number System

Decimal is a base-10 positional system with digits 0–9. The value of a digit depends on its place. Digits left of the decimal point have powers 10⁰, 10¹, 10² …; positions to its right use 10⁻¹, 10⁻² ….

The most significant digit (MSD) is the leftmost place with highest weight, and the least significant digit (LSD) is the smallest-weight place in the representation.

Expand 483.62 into weighted terms

483.62 = 4 × 10² + 8 × 10¹ + 3 × 10⁰ + 6 × 10⁻¹ + 2 × 10⁻².

The decimal point separates weights 1 and 1/10; it does not change the digits themselves.

2.1.1 · 9’s and 10’s Complements

For a fixed number of decimal places, the 9’s complement replaces each digit d with 9 − d. The 10’s complement is the 9’s complement plus one unit in the least-significant retained position.

Complements represent subtraction using addition. Decimal fractions require choosing the same number of integer and fractional columns for both operands.

Four-digit example: 2607

9’s complement of 2607 = 7392. Add one to obtain the 10’s complement = 7393.

RELATED TOPIC · NINES SUBTRACTION

2.1.2 · Subtraction with the 9’s Complement

Align operand widths. Add the 9’s complement of the subtrahend to the minuend. A carry from the most significant decimal place is wrapped around to the least-significant place, producing a positive result. When no carry occurs, complement the intermediate result and apply a negative sign.

Worked example: 704 − 286

9’s complement of 286 is 713. Add 704 + 713 = 1417. End-around carry gives 417 + 1 = 418.

End-around carry distinguishes the 9’s complement method from the 10’s complement method.

RELATED TOPIC · TENS SUBTRACTION

2.1.3 · Subtraction with the 10’s Complement

Replace the subtrahend with its 10’s complement and add. A carry means the low-order digits already contain the positive result; discard that carry. Without a carry, take the 10’s complement of the low-order sum and add a negative sign.

Worked example: 286 − 704

10’s complement of 704 is 296. Adding 286 produces 582, without a carry. The 10’s complement of 582 is 418, so the result is −418.

Binary system

2.2 · The Binary Number System

Binary has exactly two digits, 0 and 1. A bit at position k has weight 2ᵏ relative to the binary point. A group of n bits can encode 2ⁿ distinct unsigned states.

The underlying transistors operate at continuous electrical voltages; a digital circuit interprets stable voltage ranges as bit values. Binary is therefore a natural notation for switching systems.

Read 101101.01₂

Integer weights: 32 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 45. Fractional weights: 1/4 = 0.25. Hence 101101.01₂ = 45.25₁₀.

RELATED TOPIC · BINARY COUNT

2.2.1 · Counting in Binary

Binary counters increment the rightmost bit first. When a bit changes from 1 to 0, it carries into the next position. The bit patterns repeat at successively longer intervals: bit 0 every two counts, bit 1 every four, and so on.

For n unsigned bits, the range is 0 through 2ⁿ − 1; the next increment wraps to zero when the result is truncated to n bits.

2.2.2 · Binary-to-Decimal Conversion

Multiply each binary digit by its position weight and add. This works for integers and binary fractions. For integers, an equivalent left-to-right algorithm repeatedly doubles the accumulated value and adds the next bit.

Left-to-right evaluation of 11001₂

Start at 1, then compute 1×2+1=3, 3×2+0=6, 6×2+0=12, and 12×2+1=25.

RELATED TOPIC · DECIMAL TO BINARY

2.2.3 · Decimal-to-Binary Conversion

For the integer part, repeatedly divide by two and record remainders; read the remainders in reverse order. For the fractional part, repeatedly multiply by two and collect each resulting integer digit in forward order.

Not all finite decimal fractions terminate in binary. If the remainder persists, stop at a chosen precision and mark the value as an approximation.

Convert 23.625₁₀

23 is 10111₂. For 0.625: 0.625×2 = 1.25 → bit 1; 0.25×2 = 0.5 → bit 0; 0.5×2 = 1.0 → bit 1. Therefore 23.625₁₀ = 10111.101₂.

RELATED TOPIC · BINARY FRACTIONS

2.2.3 · Fractional Radix Conversion

A fractional digit to the right of the radix point has weight b⁻ᵏ. To produce digits from a decimal fraction, multiply the fractional remainder by the target base, take the integer part as the next digit, and continue with the new fraction.

Stopping after a fixed number of bits gives a truncated approximation, not necessarily an exact conversion. Increase precision to reduce quantization error.

Why 0.1₁₀ repeats in binary

Successive multiplication by 2 produces the recurring pattern 000110011… . No finite binary expansion is exactly 0.1 in decimal.

Binary arithmetic

2.2.4 · Binary Addition

Add one column at a time from right to left. For each bit, the output sum is the current-column total modulo two; any value of two or more generates a carry to the next column.

A one-bit full adder computes sum = A ⊕ B ⊕ Cin and Cout = AB + Cin(A ⊕ B). Cascading full adders implements a multi-bit adder.

Add 1011₂ + 0110₂

11 + 6 = 17, so the untruncated five-bit result is 10001₂. A four-bit register would retain only 0001 and flag a carry out.

2.2.5 · Binary Subtraction

Subtract each position right to left. When the minuend bit cannot supply the required quantity, borrow one from the next position, adding two to the current column.

In digital hardware, subtraction is often implemented using the adder with the second operand inverted and a carry-in of 1—equivalent to two’s complement subtraction.

Subtract 1101₂ − 0111₂

13 − 7 = 6, so the four-bit output is 0110₂. A larger borrow flag depends on the subtraction architecture.

2.2.6 · Binary Multiplication

Each multiplier bit controls a partial product. A zero contributes zero; a one contributes a shifted copy of the multiplicand. Adding the shifted rows produces the result.

An n-bit unsigned factor multiplied by another n-bit unsigned factor may require a 2n-bit result.

1011₂ × 0101₂

11 × 5 = 55 = 00110111₂. The active multiplier bits (bit 0 and bit 2) contribute 1011 + 101100.

A hardware multiplier may use registers and an adder instead of generating every partial product at once. It examines multiplier bits in order, adds the appropriate shifted multiplicand, then advances the shift state.

2.2.8 · Binary Division

Unsigned division compares a partial dividend to the divisor, subtracts when possible, and emits the next quotient bit. The remainder is what remains after removing the full divisor multiples.

For nonzero divisor b, the integers satisfy a = bq + r with 0 ≤ r < b.

Divide 110110₂ by 101₂

54 ÷ 5 = 10 remainder 4. Thus the quotient is 1010₂ and the remainder is 0100₂.

2.2.9 · Hardware Division Registers

A restoring divider maintains a partial remainder register and a developing quotient. At each step it shifts in another dividend bit, attempts a subtraction, and restores the remainder when the subtraction would go negative.

Signed numbers

2.3 · Signed Number Representations

Signed numbers need an encoding for negative values. Three historical schemes are sign-magnitude, one’s complement, and two’s complement. Positive values generally share the same binary encoding; negative values differ.

Sign-magnitude stores a sign bit and magnitude. One’s complement flips every bit for a negative value. Two’s complement flips bits and adds one, supporting a unique zero and straightforward addition in finite-width hardware.

Bit width is not optional in signed arithmetic: the same bit pattern can represent different quantities under different signed conventions.
Three signed-number encodings
RELATED TOPIC · TWOS REPR

2.3.1 · Two’s Complement Representation

For an n-bit signed two’s complement word, representable values run from −2ⁿ⁻¹ to +2ⁿ⁻¹ − 1. The most significant bit is 1 for negative values and 0 for nonnegative values.

To encode a negative value, write the positive magnitude in n bits, complement every bit, then add one modulo 2ⁿ.

Encode −18 with eight bits

+18 = 00010010; inverted = 11101101; plus 1 = 11101110. The signed value of 11101110₂ is −18.

Signed numbers

2.3.2 · Two’s Complement Arithmetic

Add n-bit operands just as unsigned bit strings and retain only the low n bits. Interpret the resulting word as a signed value. Subtraction computes A + (two’s complement of B).

Signed overflow occurs if the mathematically correct answer lies outside the n-bit signed range; an ordinary carry-out by itself does not determine signed overflow.

Eight-bit operation: 46 − 14

00101110 + 11110010 = (1)00100000. Discard the ninth carry bit: 00100000₂ = 32.

Signed arithmetic in fixed-width hardware
RELATED TOPIC · ONES REPR

One’s Complement Representation

One’s complement represents a negative value by inverting each bit of its positive n-bit magnitude. In an eight-bit word, +7 is 00000111 and −7 is 11111000.

Unlike two’s complement, one’s complement has two representations of zero: all zeros and all ones. This affects arithmetic and comparisons.

Encode −29 in eight bits

+29 = 00011101. Flip every bit to obtain 11100010.

RELATED TOPIC · ONES ARITHMETIC

2.3.3 · One’s Complement Arithmetic

Add one’s-complement operands as bit strings. If an extra carry leaves the word, add that carry to the least significant bit. This is known as end-around carry.

For a negative result, invert the stored bits to recover its magnitude; remember that two different all-equal patterns encode zero.

Eight-bit −5 + 9

−5 = 11111010, +9 = 00001001. Add = (1)00000011; add end-around carry to obtain 00000100 = +4.

2.3.4 · Double-Precision Integers

When a result exceeds one storage word, combine two fixed-width words into a wider representation. A 16-bit high word followed by a 16-bit low word can hold one 32-bit quantity. Carry propagation between words is essential for unsigned or multiword additions.

“Double precision” here means an integer represented across two hardware words; it is distinct from modern IEEE-754 double-precision floating point.

Split a 32-bit word

0x1234ABCD consists of high word 0x1234 and low word 0xABCD.

2.3.5 · Floating-Point Representation

Floating-point formats store a significand (mantissa) and exponent, allowing a wide dynamic range. A simplified binary value can be described as sign × significand × 2ᵉ.

Octal and hexadecimal

2.4 · The Octal Number System

Octal uses digits 0–7 with base 8. Because 8 = 2³, each octal digit corresponds to exactly three binary bits. Octal is a compact way to inspect long binary values.

Binary grouping: 110101001₂

Group from the radix point: 110 101 001 → 6 5 1. Therefore 110101001₂ = 651₈.

Binary grouping into nibbles or triads
RELATED TOPIC · OCTAL CONVERSION

2.4.2–2.4.5 · Octal Conversions

To change octal to binary, replace each octal digit with its three-bit counterpart. To change binary to octal, group bits in threes outward from the binary point, padding end groups with zeros when needed.

Conversion between decimal and octal uses ordinary positional weights or repeated division/multiplication with base 8.

Convert 27.4₈

2×8 + 7 + 4/8 = 23.5₁₀; in binary it is 010 111 . 100₂, or 10111.1₂.

Octal and hexadecimal

2.4.6 · Octal Arithmetic

Arithmetic in base 8 uses carries and borrows at eight rather than ten. Octal subtraction can also use 7’s complement and 8’s complement, analogous to decimal complement methods.

Converting both operands to binary, performing the operation, and converting back is often convenient in digital systems.

Octal addition: 57₈ + 24₈

57₈ = 47₁₀ and 24₈ = 20₁₀. Their sum, 67₁₀, is 103₈.

Octal and hexadecimal

2.5 · The Hexadecimal Number System

Hexadecimal is base 16 with digits 0–9 and A–F, where A=10 through F=15. Because 16 = 2⁴, one hexadecimal digit exactly represents a four-bit nibble.

Hexadecimal is widely used for memory addresses, bit masks, register values and byte dumps. The notation 0x3A represents three sixteens plus ten, or 58 decimal.

Convert 11010110₂ to hexadecimal

Group into 1101 0110 → D 6, so the number is D6₁₆.

2.5.1–2.5.5 · Hexadecimal Conversions

Count through digits 0,1,…9,A,B,…F, then advance the next place after F. Convert hexadecimal to binary by mapping every digit to four bits. Convert from decimal by repeated division by 16 (integer part) or multiplication by 16 (fractional part).

Convert A4F₁₆

10×16² + 4×16 + 15 = 2639₁₀. Each digit expands to 1010 0100 1111₂.

2.5.6–2.5.7 · Octal ↔ Hexadecimal

To translate directly between octal and hexadecimal, use binary as a common intermediate representation. Expand octal digits in groups of three bits and regroup into four-bit nibbles for hexadecimal, or reverse the process.

Convert 753₈ to hexadecimal

7 5 3 → 111 101 011₂. Regroup 0001 1110 1011 → 1EB₁₆.

RELATED TOPIC · HEX ARITHMETIC

2.5.8 · Hexadecimal Arithmetic

Hexadecimal addition and subtraction follow the same positional principles as other bases, with carry at sixteen. Complement-based subtraction may use 15’s complement or 16’s complement for fixed-width words.

A digital circuit may perform the arithmetic in its binary datapath while hexadecimal serves as a readable presentation of bit groups.

Hexadecimal addition: 2B₁₆ + 1E₁₆

43 + 30 = 73₁₀, represented as 49₁₆.
