Minimization of Switching Functions

A switching function describes which input combinations produce HIGH and LOW outputs. Minimization preserves every specified response while replacing a long Boolean equation with a smaller collection of logical conditions.

Explore each Karnaugh-map cell, observe how groups eliminate changing variables, and compare SOP, POS and logic-gate implementation choices.

RELATED TOPIC · FOUNDATIONS

Why minimize a switching function?

Canonical expressions describe a function completely, but often repeat literals and require unnecessary gates. A reduced expression can lower the number of product terms, gate inputs and interconnect connections.

The procedure starts from a truth table: group combinations that differ in only one bit, remove those changing literals and cover all required output states.

6.1 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

Minterms, maxterms and complete specifications

An n-variable function has 2ⁿ input combinations. A minterm identifies one combination where F = 1; a maxterm identifies one combination where F = 0.

The notation Σm(1,3,5) lists asserted minterms. The complementary zero positions can be written ΠM(0,2,4,6,7), assuming a three-variable function.

RELATED TOPIC · SOP

Sum-of-products and minterm notation

SOP collects product terms with OR. Each K-map 1 is a required minterm; adjacent 1 cells may be grouped into pairs, quads or octets.

Within a group, a variable that changes between 0 and 1 disappears from the product term. The bits that remain fixed determine the literal polarities.

RELATED TOPIC · POS

Product-of-sums and maxterm notation

POS joins sum terms using AND. A K-map zero is a maxterm, and groups of adjacent zeros produce shorter sum terms.

When deriving a POS term, a fixed 0 contributes the uncomplemented variable; a fixed 1 contributes the complemented variable.

6.2 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

Two-variable Karnaugh map

For variables A and B the map has four cells, representing m₀ through m₃. Two cells share an edge only when their bit patterns differ in one position.

Selecting cells m₀, m₂ and m₃ creates a function equivalent to ¬B + A; the map exposes those two groups directly.

RELATED TOPIC · TWO SOP

Two-variable SOP minimization

Make pairs of adjacent 1 cells before retaining an isolated single cell. When every cell is 1, no variable remains and the function is simply 1.

Overlapping groups are allowed when a shared cell helps eliminate more literals.

Two-variable POS minimization

The same truth table may require a different number of literals in minimized SOP and POS forms.

6.3 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

Three-variable K-map and Gray order

A three-variable map contains eight cells. The column sequence 00, 01, 11, 10 is Gray ordered; every adjacent column differs by a single bit.

Its first and last columns are adjacent even though they appear on opposite sides of the drawing.

Reading a group into a Boolean term

For an SOP group, scan each variable across the highlighted cells. Write ¬A if A stays 0, write A if A stays 1, and omit A entirely if it changes.

For a POS zero-group, the polarity is reversed within the sum term. Test any resulting equation against the table.

RELATED TOPIC · WRAP

Wrap-around and overlapping groups

The left and right edges of a K-map touch, as do the top and bottom. Four corner cells may therefore form one legal quad.

Diagonal neighbors cannot be grouped unless they are also connected through Gray-code adjacency. A valid group is always a power of two in size.

The explorer lists each maximal group, the input indices it covers and the literal term produced by its unchanged variables.

RELATED TOPIC · ESSENTIAL

Essential and selective prime implicants

An essential prime implicant covers at least one required output-1 cell that no other prime implicant covers. Such a group must appear in every prime-implicant cover.

Nonessential primes can offer alternative ways to complete the cover. Compare their literal counts before choosing.

False prime implicants for POS

When solving POS, the maximal groups are formed from zero cells. These are sometimes called false prime implicants because they cover the false-set of the function.

Essential zero-groups are those uniquely covering a required zero. Their expressions become sum factors.

RELATED TOPIC · DONTCARE

Don’t-care inputs and incompletely specified functions

An input combination that never occurs or whose output is irrelevant can be marked X. The minimizer may treat X as 0 or 1, whichever helps simplify the equation.

A don’t-care cell is optional: it cannot be used to force an unneeded output, and no expression must specifically cover X.

Variable-entered K-maps

A variable-entered map uses a map cell to display the remaining variable or a function of that variable. It can reduce how much map space is needed for five- and six-variable functions.

6.12 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

Practical limits of Karnaugh maps

K-maps are intuitive up to about six variables. Above that, the number of cells and candidate overlapping groups becomes difficult to inspect manually.

Tabular minimization and computer algorithms replace visual grouping for larger input counts and dense functions.

Two-level AND–OR and OR–AND logic

A minimized SOP naturally maps to parallel AND gates followed by an OR gate. A minimized POS maps to parallel OR gates followed by one AND gate.

Inverting bubbles change the polarity of each physical stage but not the input/output truth table.

6.14.1 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

AND–OR–INVERT structures

An AOI gate computes the complement of a sum of products, such as Y = ¬(AB + CD). The final inversion is integrated into the output stage.

Gate libraries commonly provide AOI cells, so a factored expression can sometimes use fewer stages than a separate AND/OR/inverter chain.

OR–AND–INVERT structures

An OAI gate computes the complement of a product of sums, such as Y = ¬[(A+B)(C+D)]. This is the dual of an AOI-style structure.

The surrounding logic polarity determines whether an AOI or OAI cell is a convenient implementation choice.

6.15 · SWITCHING FUNCTIONS

Quine–McCluskey tabular minimization

Quine–McCluskey replaces drawing cells with a systematic combination process. Start with minterms grouped by their number of 1 bits; combine terms that differ in one position.

Continue combining until no further valid merges are possible, then build a prime-implicant chart to choose a covering set.

A cube can combine again with another cube with a matching dash position and one additional differing bit.

Verilog implementation and verification

A sum of products can be coded with bitwise & and | operators in a continuous assignment. A case statement can describe the original truth table for an independent check.

Treat don’t-care outputs deliberately; simulation X values are not a substitute for confirming the real hardware requirements.
